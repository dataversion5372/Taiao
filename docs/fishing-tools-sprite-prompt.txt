ISLE OF EMBERFALL — FISHING TOOLS ICON PROMPT
=============================================
AUDIT: the five Fishing-skill tools (fishing_rod, small_net, big_net,
harpoon, lobster_cage — data.js) all currently share ONE placeholder icon
(i_rod = a single rod sprite), so a net, a harpoon and a lobster cage are
indistinguishable in the pack, shop and skill guide. This sheet replaces
them with real per-tool art. Integration follows the "gi" item-icon
pattern (tools/build_item_icons.py -> item-icons-data.js): slice the sheet
into 64px cells left-to-right, pack, and repoint ITEMS[id].icon for the
five ids below.

==============================================================================
GLOBAL STYLE
==============================================================================
Art style: 16-bit / 'HD-2D' pixel-art inventory icons in the look of the
roguelike NES RPGs and classic RuneScape item sprites. One tool per cell,
centred and filling most of the cell, drawn 3/4 or flat side-on with a
bold readable silhouette, a thin dark outline, a limited flat palette with
a little dithered shading, and a soft light from the upper-left. Weathered,
salt-worn fishing gear — pale ash/oak wood, tarred cord and rope, dull
grey wrought iron. Fully TRANSPARENT background (alpha), no drop shadows,
no text, numbers, labels, borders or frames. Consistent scale, lighting
and palette across the whole sheet so the five icons read as one matched
set. Unlike a tiered sheet, every cell is a DIFFERENT tool — each must be
instantly recognisable at 32x32 from its silhouette alone.

Output: one PNG sprite sheet, a uniform grid of 5 columns x 1 row, 64x64 px
per cell (draw at 64x64; the game downscales to 32x32), one icon per cell,
even margins, no gridlines drawn, in the EXACT order below.

==============================================================================
SHEET: Fishing tools   |   Skill: Fishing   |   Grid: 5 cols x 1 row (5 icons)
==============================================================================
Draw these five tools, left-to-right, each a distinct instantly-recognisable
icon in the global style:

  1. fishing_rod  — a slender tapering wooden fishing rod/pole held on the
     diagonal (butt lower-left, tip upper-right), a thin taut line running
     from the tip with a small red-and-white bobber and a tiny barbed hook.

  2. small_net    — a small round hand landing-net: a bent wooden hoop with
     knotted string mesh sagging in a shallow bag, on a short wooden handle
     angled diagonally. Reads as light and one-handed.

  3. big_net      — a large heavy seine/trawl net: a wide draped sheet of
     knotted mesh gathered into folds, strung with a top row of pale round
     cork floats and a bottom row of dark lead weights. Clearly bigger and
     bulkier than the small net.

  4. harpoon      — a wrought-iron fishing harpoon/spear held on the diagonal
     (point to the upper-right): a long barbed leaf-shaped iron head with a
     back-swept barb, a socketed wooden haft, and a coil of tarred throwing
     rope looped at the base.

  5. lobster_cage — a woven creel lobster pot: a rounded barrel/dome trap of
     bent willow withies or wire bars with a funnel entrance in the front,
     a small rope handle on top, and a fish scrap visible as bait inside.
     Reads as a cage, not a basket of goods.

INTEGRATION (after the PNG is generated):
  - Add the sheet to tools/build_item_icons.py in the "gi"/fishing group,
    keeping the 5-cell order above; it packs 64px cells into item-icons.
  - Repoint the icons: fishing_rod, small_net, big_net, harpoon,
    lobster_cage -> their new per-tool cells (drop the shared i_rod).
